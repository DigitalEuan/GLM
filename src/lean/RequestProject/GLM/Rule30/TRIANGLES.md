## The owner's observations (Round 48)

> A line heading left and down is always continuous; I only see the odd line heading right and
> down, and then ending. Larger white upside-down triangles appear more on the right side and are
> not seen near the left edge at all. They look like a determining operator to me: a cellular

Each part has an exact counterpart. Some parts hold exactly as stated, some need a correction, and
one part (are the left-side triangles bounded?) is still open.

Coordinates: `a(t)(x)` is the cell at time `t`, position `x`. The pattern from one black cell
fills `|x| ≤ t`. A **left diagonal** `ldiag D t = a(t)(D − t)` is the line heading left and down,
`D` cells inside the left edge. A **right diagonal** `rdiag D t = a(t)(t − D)` is the line heading
right and down, `D` cells inside the right edge.

## 1. What a white triangle is (exact, for every row)

| Statement | Lean |
|---|---|
| A white run `[a, b]` is followed by the white run `[a+1, b−1]`. After `k` steps it is `[a+k, b−k]`. A white run of length `L` is the top of a white triangle of height `⌈L/2⌉`, **whatever the rest of the row is** | `white_shrinks`, `white_triangle` |
| If the run has black cells on both sides, the triangle is outlined in black in every row. Its left side is a black line heading right and down, its right side a black line heading left and down | `white_run_black_borders`, `maximal_white_triangle` |
| Tips: one white cell between two blacks stays white (odd top → white tip). Two whites between blacks become two blacks (even top → closed bottom) | `odd_tip_white`, `even_tip_closes` |
| **Birth.** Above a white run `[a, b]` the cells `[a−1, b−1]` of the previous row all have the same colour. If they are all **black**, a new triangle is born from that black run. If they are all **white**, the run is just the next row of a bigger triangle | `white_run_parent` |
| A black run `[a, b]` is followed by the white run `[a+1, b]` | `black_run_child` |

So the owner's "operator" reading is right in this exact sense. A triangle is **completely fixed by
its top row**: nothing outside it can change its inside or its outline. Every triangle is
**generated by a black run in the row just before**, at least as long as the triangle's top. The
size of the next triangle is set by the previous row: **a black run of length `L` makes a white
triangle with top `L − 1`**.

## 2. Left-down versus right-down lines (exact, for every row)

| Statement | Lean |
|---|---|
| A black cell continues a **right-down** line iff **both** cells to its right are white | `rightDown_continues_iff` |
| A black cell continues a **left-down** line iff the **one** cell two to its left is white (the cell directly left of it does not matter) | `leftDown_continues_iff` |

A right-down black line can only run along the left side of a white region: these lines **are**
the left outlines of the white triangles, and they stop where the triangle stops. A left-down line
needs only one white cell, so it continues twice as often. Measured (EMP, rows `< 2000`): finished
left-down segments have mean length `1.99`, right-down ones `1.33`. These are the values `2` and
`4/3` expected for continuation chances `1/2` and `1/4`.

**Lines that never end (the seed pattern, exact):**

| Statement | Lean |
|---|---|
| For `D ≥ 2`: left-down line `D` is eventually all black **iff** left diagonal `D − 2` is eventually all white | `ldiag_eventuallyBlack_iff` |
| **Infinitely many left-down black lines never end** | `ldiag_infinitely_many_black` |
| Lines `0` and `1` (the doubled left edge) are black | `ldiag_zero_one_black` |
| **Every right-down black line other than the right edge breaks** within every `2^D` steps (`D` = depth) | `rdiag_black_segment_short`, `rdiag_not_eventuallyBlack` |
| The right edge itself never breaks | `rdiag_zero_black` |

With the certified white diagonals `2, 7, 28, 399, 53207, 58286, 87866` (Round 38), the left-down
lines that never end include `0, 1, 4, 9, 30, 401, 53209, 58288, 87868`. The observation "a line
heading left and down is always continuous" needs one correction: not every left-down line goes on
forever (most break after about two cells). But infinitely many of them do, while no right-down
line inside the pattern does.

## 3. The print-out from the right side in (exact)

* `column_from_right`: `a(t)(c − 1) = a(t+1)(c) xor (a(t)(c) ∨ a(t)(c+1))`. Columns `c` and
  `c + 1` (over all time) fix column `c − 1`. So the pattern can be **printed from the right side in,
  one column at a time**. The reverse does not work, because the rule cannot be solved for its right
  input. This is the same one-way flow as the rigid right side of the influence cone
  (`ConeGeometry.lean`) and the sideways reading (`SidewaysView.lean`).

## 4. The bands at the right edge (exact)

| Statement | Lean |
|---|---|
| The first `k` right diagonals repeat with period `2^k`. Near the right edge, the rows `[n·2^k, (n+1)·2^k)` are a copy of the rows `[0, 2^k)` | `right_band_copy` |
| At every row `t = n·2^k`, the `k` cells just inside the right edge are white | `right_edge_white_run` |
| … so a white triangle with top `k` hangs from the right edge there | `right_edge_triangle` |
| White triangles of **every** size occur one cell inside the right edge | `arbitrarily_large_right_triangles` |

This is an exact version of "bands printed from the right with large white triangles at their
start, generated by the previous band". The right edge strip is made of bands of length `2, 4, 8,
…`. Each band of length `2^k` begins with a white triangle with top at least `k`, and inside the
strip each band repeats the bands before it. The size of the right-edge triangle at row `t` is at
least the number of times `2` divides `t`. Measured (EMP): the record right-edge triangles are at
rows `2, 4, 8, …, 8192` exactly, with tops `2, 3, 5, 6, 8, 14, 15, 23, 24, 26, 28, 33, 35`, about
`2.6·k` at row `2^k`. The proved lower bound is `k`.

## 5. The left edge: no large triangles (certified)

| Statement | Lean |
|---|---|
| Inside the first `400` left diagonals (and the light cone) there is no white run of length `16` at **any** time | `left400_no_window_16` |
| From time `501` on, the longest white run there is exactly `10` (reached at `t = 505 + 8n`, diagonals `227–236`) | `left400_settled_no_window_11`, `left400_settled_window_10` |
| From time `26834` on, the longest white run inside the first `20000` left diagonals is exactly `16` (reached at `t = 26840 + 16n`, diagonals `2347–2362`) | `left20000_settled_no_window_17`, `left20000_settled_window_16` |

Compare: at every row `n·2^20000`, the `20000` cells just inside the **right** edge are all white.

Measured over the whole row (EMP, rows `100 ≤ t < 10000`, centre of the run by eighths of the row,
left to right):

| | 1st | 2nd | 3rd | 4th | 5th | 6th | 7th | 8th |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| longest white run | 16 | 16 | 16 | 23 | 20 | 21 | 20 | 35 |
| runs of length `≥ 20` | 0 | 0 | 0 | 7 | 1 | 4 | 1 | 119 |

So the large triangles really do cluster at the right edge. In the left three eighths nothing
longer than `16` appears up to row `10000`. Runs of length `8` are equally common everywhere.

**Correction to "not seen near the left edge at all".** Triangles are not absent near the left
edge, they are **small** there. The left strip settles into a periodic pattern (period `8` for
`D < 400`, `16` for `D < 87867`) with runs up to `10`, and `16`, respectively. Whether the longest
settled white run near the left edge stays bounded as the strip widens is **open** (To Do 52).
The widths seen so far (`10` at `400`, `16` at `20000`) grow very slowly.

## 6. For the GLM

* **The triangle is an operator with a known input**: the black run in the previous row. Its
  output (white inside, black outline, tip type) is fully determined. A GLM reader can treat each
  white triangle as one token: (row, position, top length) → a fixed shape.
* **An exact arrow.** Information can be carried right to left (`column_from_right`) but not left
  to right. The right edge is periodic in bands (`right_band_copy`), and the left edge settles into
  short periods (Rounds 12, 38). This gives the "which way are we looking" direction that the owner
  asked the wobble to supply. The observer that sees everything is the one reading **from the right
  edge inwards**.
* **What this does not do.** None of these statements is about the centre column. The centre column
  crosses every left and right diagonal once, inside their transients. The three prize problems
  remain open.
