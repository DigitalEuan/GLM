## 1. The four constructions, and why they are exact

For every k with k² > 1000 (k = 32..99), all on the integer lattice with
unit squares centred at (x + ½, y + ½):

| kind | n | design L | validity |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| `square k^2` | k² | k | the k × k lattice |
| `k^2-1` … `k^2-4` | k²−i, i = 1..4 | k | the lattice with i squares removed from the last row; **optimal** by T-084 (i=1), T-086 (i=2), T-064 (i=3), T-081 (i=4), and the area bound at i=0 |
| `k^2+j (new row)` | k²+j, j = 1..k | k+1 | the lattice plus j squares in a row above it — an upper bound, not claimed optimal |

**The validity argument is elementary and exact.** Two axis-aligned unit
squares with *integer* centres overlap in their interiors only if their
centres coincide, because distinct integer points differ by at least 1 on some
axis. So the whole check is "all centres distinct", O(n), no tolerance
anywhere. `--gate` then hands 24 spread states to `packlib.finalize` +
`packlib.verify`, the project's authority, to confirm that the elementary
argument and the gate agree.

## 3. What this is, and what it is not

**It is** the answer to "are some of those rows easy": more than half of the
9,000-row window is constructible in closed form, 340 of those rows are
*provably optimal*, and every one passes the gate. The covered half is the
**lower half of every band** (j ≤ k of each `k² … (k+1)²` block).

**It is not** a bound on s(n) for n > 1000, it does not change the score (the
scoring is n ≤ 1000, and `submission.csv` is untouched), and the `k^2+j` rows
are upper bounds only.

**The uncovered 47% is the interesting half.** Those are n = k² + j with
j > k — the upper half of each band, where `L = k+1` stops being plausible and
a real arrangement is needed. Nothing in this tree says anything about them.

## 4. The uncomfortable corollary about training sets

The census also answers the training-set question by measurement rather than
assumption. Every easy row above 1000 is a **lattice** row: 100% of them are
axis-aligned unit lattices by construction, and the 340 proved-optimal ones
have median depth 0.0000–0.0005. Widening the window using the *easy* rows
therefore widens **the vacuum**, not the vocabulary: it adds 4,454 rows whose
contacts all sit in the two lattice relation cells that the run-40 census
already showed are 99.99% of the proved end of the table.

That is not an argument against the wider window — it is an argument about
what it can be used for. It gives (a) a much larger sample of the vacuum
phase, (b) the band structure and the exact depth ladder above 1000, and
(c) a clean statement of where the non-vacuum work lives (the upper half of
every band). It does not give a training set for the seam vocabulary, because
the easy rows have no seams.
