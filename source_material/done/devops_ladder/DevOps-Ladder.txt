### What you’re proposing
You’re suggesting that if GLM internalises the DevOps ladder (Tier 0 machine substrate → Tier 1 shell/control → … → Tier 9 culture) as its native vocabulary, then “thinking” becomes the composition and projection of those discrete, hierarchical carriers.  


That would turn reasoning into something closer to a formal language-of-thought: deterministic steps built from operational primitives (process, pipe, exit code, gate, artifact, SLO, feedback loop, etc.) rather than free-form natural language. Because GLM already encodes meanings as points in a 24-dimensional Golay/Leech substrate with exact arithmetic, the ladder could become a set of geometric registers that support genuine compositional, lossless reasoning about processes.


### Has anyone done this already?


**Exact match: No.**  
I found no public work that takes a layered DevOps-style operational ontology (machine primitives → control flow → config → artifacts → pipelines → infrastructure → observability → reliability → culture) and installs it as the internal thought language of an AI system, especially a geometric/exact one like GLM.


**Closest existing directions:**


- **Hierarchical / structured reasoning scaffolds**  
  Hierarchical Chain-of-Thought, Ladders-of-Thought, Tree/Graph of Thoughts, and related work force LLMs to reason in staged or hierarchical steps rather than flat text. These improve coherence and efficiency, but they still operate in natural language, not a fixed technical vocabulary of operational primitives.


- **Ontologies and taxonomies as grounding for agents**  
  There is growing work on operational ontologies for AI agents (executable models of entities, relations, and permitted actions). These are used to constrain what an agent is allowed to do and to make decisions more reliable. They are usually domain-specific (business processes, enterprise systems) and sit outside the model as guardrails or retrieval structures, not as the model’s internal language of thought.


- **Language-of-Thought ideas applied to LLMs**  
  Recent papers explicitly discuss the gap between natural language and a true “language of thoughts,” and propose prompting techniques (Language-of-Thoughts / LoT) that try to force more structured, premise-reordered reasoning. Still natural-language based.


- **Neuro-symbolic and compositional systems**  
  Multiple lines of work encode discrete concepts or hierarchical primitives and compose them (neuro-symbolic concept learners, hierarchical rule induction, constrained object hierarchies, etc.). These are the closest in spirit — discrete, compositional, hierarchical — but none use a DevOps operational ladder as the concept set, and almost none are built on an exact geometric substrate like Golay/Leech.


- **AgentOps / Agentic DevOps**  
  There is a literature around “AgentOps” and “Agentic DevOps,” but these are about applying DevOps practices *to* agents (observability, deployment, lifecycle management of agents), not about making agents think *in* DevOps primitives.


### Why this could work especially well with GLM


GLM already has several properties that make the ladder a natural fit:


- Concepts live as discrete carriers on a mathematically exact substrate → the ladder tiers can be encoded as distinct geometric registers.
- Composition and analogy are native operations (stacking, relay, conjugate registers, projections).
- The system already measures information loss and capability boundaries → you can detect when a higher-tier concept is being used without sufficient lower-tier grounding.
- Exact arithmetic and refusal discipline align with the deterministic, “no silent failure” ethos of good DevOps.


In short: most current systems either (a) stay in free-form language or (b) use external structured knowledge. You are proposing to make the structured operational hierarchy the *internal medium of thought* itself, inside a geometric reasoner. That combination appears to be novel.


### Bottom line
The hypothesis is sound and sits at the intersection of several active research threads (hierarchical reasoning, operational ontologies, language-of-thought, neuro-symbolic composition). No one seems to have implemented precisely this version yet — a DevOps-style ladder as the native thought vocabulary of a lattice-based geometric system. That makes it a clean, high-leverage experiment for GLM.


If you want to push further, the next concrete step would be to define a minimal set of carriers for the lower tiers (0–2 or 0–4) and test whether GLM can perform simple compositional queries (e.g., “what is the geometric relation between a pipe and a feedback loop?”) more cleanly than with unconstrained language.
