# The open candidates, merged and ordered: which developments are one piece of work, and which one supports the next

## Tier 0 — the coarse read

**Question.** Of the items `STATUS.md` §3.4 names for the next round, which are the same piece of work under different names, which depend on which, and in what order should they be taken so that each round leaves the next one cheaper?

**Verdict.** The 49 open items reduce to 7 tracks of development plus a set of items gated on the owner or on outside material; the measurand register is taken first because six items are that one piece of work and three later tracks read it, and the loop through the planner is taken second because four items are that one piece of work and it is what the standing target calls scarce.

**Deciding figure.** 49 open items; 6 of them are the measurand register, 4 the loop, 4 the discourse state, 2 the third sort, 2 the held-out set; 7 tracks; 6 items gated on the owner or on outside material, 2 proposed for retirement.

**Recomputed by.** (hand-written argument; nothing to recompute)

*Tier 0 is a coarse read of what follows, never a claim of its own: the verdict and the figure above are grounded in the body below, and `glm_universal.corpus.checks.tier_report` fails if they stop being.*

---

## 0. Where this comes from

The owner asked, at the start of Phase 87, for the remaining development
tasks to be read together: whether some can be combined, and whether there is
a logical order in which one development supports the next.
[`STATUS.md`](../STATUS.md) §3.4 had grown by accretion — each round named
what it left, under the letter of the candidate it came from — so the same
piece of work now appears under two or three letters, and the order of the
list is the order the items were *named*, not the order they can be *built*.

This document is an argument, not a measurement: nothing here is recomputed,
and no item is claimed done by being placed. What it changes is the order of
§3.4 and the unit a round takes.

## 2. The merges: one piece of work under several names

**The measurand register — 6 items: K2, O5b, O5c, O5d, 1a, 1b.** K2 asks for
the junction table to become a register of measurands *so that a conversion
through a stated efficiency is a law*; that is O5c. O5b is the same
register read from the planner's side (a register measurand feeding a wheel
quantity by name), and §3.4 already says O5b *is* K2 and the second half of
candidate 1 (1b). O5d — the elementary charge as a supplied constant — is one
more row of the constants Phase 86 put in `runtime/measurands.py`, which is
the module the register extends. 1a — an offset row in the scale table — is
the one half of `GLM.ScaleConversion.cmpQ_apply` that is proved and not
shipped; Phase 86 already reads Celsius with its offset in the planner, so
the scale table's offset row is the same declaration seen from the
comparison operation. One round, one declaration, six items.

**The loop through the planner — 4 items: O3, M3, K4, I2.** O3 and M3 are the
same sentence in two candidates (*the planner's answer chooses the next
reverse operation*). K4 makes a derivation a value a program can hold; I2
makes a program's expression a question the planner can hold. Together they
are the two directions of one bridge between the dialect and the planner, and
O3/M3 is the loop that runs across it. L9 (the planner's utility gate) is not
part of the build, but it is the measurement the loop should be re-read
against: it asks for a tool the kind-driven dispatcher cannot reach, and the
loop is the first candidate for one.

**The discourse state — 4 items: D, 0a, 0b, K3.** D and 0a are the same item
(the fourth shape of follow-up). 0b — a tie carried forward as a column —
needs *a statement of what the answer for several rows at once is*; Phases 84
and 85 built exactly that for the stepwise planner (folds and order statistics
over a declared set, with the missing rows named), so 0b now has its
prerequisite. K3 (follow-ups on every surface) is the same state carried past
the planner.

**The third sort — 2 items: I1, M1.** Strings, tuples, lists and ranges are
one sort; I1 asks the evaluator to read it and M1 asks the reverse sentences
to say it. Built once, with one declaration of the sort, both sides read it.
M2 (an imperative grammar for the 7 programs with state) follows it.

**The held-out set — 2 items: B, O1.** O1 is B sharpened to the planner.
L4 (a register nobody constructed) is the same *kind* of requirement — material
from outside the project — for the retrieval side, and is gated with them.

**The second reading — J2 and H-X1.** Both ask for a second, independent view
of one carrier so that a second reading is routine rather than supplied by
the caller; H-X1 is J2 applied to the deep-hole fork. They are listed in one
track (§3, track 6) rather than counted as a merge, because the deep-hole
instance has its own carrier.

## 3. The tracks, and what each supports

Each track is a sequence; an arrow is *supports* — the later item is cheaper,
or only well-posed, once the earlier one exists.

1. **Measurands** — the measurand register (K2 + O5b + O5c + O5d + 1a + 1b)
   → F (typed operators: real, reactive and apparent power are three
   measurands of one dimension, and the volt-ampere and the var are
   kind-restricted names of exactly the sort `runtime/measurands.py` already
   holds for the hertz and the newton metre) → C + H1 (a discrepancy report
   needs each register column to say *which* measurand it holds and which
   standard it is to be read against; H1's two failing completion rules are
   columns of the same register, and demoting them is the same audit read
   from the completion side).
2. **The loop** — K4 → I2 → O3/M3, then L9 re-read. The register of track 1
   makes `derive` more useful (a register value can feed a derivation by
   name), which is why this track comes second rather than first.
3. **Planner widenings** — H-E6 → O7r + 2r. Every widening of Phases 73–85
   was a hand-written frame; generating frames from a declaration first turns
   the molecule table, neutrons, further prefixes, top-*k* and quantiles into
   declarations rather than code.
4. **Discourse state** — 0b → D/0a → K3. 0b first, because its prerequisite
   (an answer over several rows) now exists and it turns the conversation
   layer's sharpest refusal into an answer.
5. **The third sort** — I1 + M1 → M2.
6. **Second readings and the lattice** — J2 (with H-X1) → J1 → J3. J3 is now
   well-posed: Phase 82 estimates the channel's rate from the machine's own
   reads ([`RATE_POSTERIOR_STUDY.md`](RATE_POSTERIOR_STUDY.md)), which is the
   reliability J3 asks for in place of a declared formula. The lattice items
   L6, L10, L7 and L3 sit beside this track; L6 and L10 are theorems to be
   attempted in Lean, L7 and L3 need new pre-registrations.
7. **Retrieval** — N1 → L5 → N2, N5, I3. N1 first: it says whether the
   single-book figures N2 and N5 would refine are effects or draws, and
   should be known before effort goes into refining them. L5 is a small,
   bounded fix to the feature map the same rankings read.

**Standalone, small:** L8 (a margin sweep for the adopted guard), H-E4, H-E7.
They fit at the tail of any round.

**Gated on the owner (3):** K1, P-a, P-b — each is a change of contract.
**Gated on outside material (3):** B, O1, L4. These 6 are not development
rounds until the decision or the material arrives.

**Proposed for retirement (2):** P-c, because the GLM does not implement the
UBP pipelines those laws would have to be read through; H-c6, because it
waits on an object no candidate proposes to build. G stays on the list at the
lowest priority: no code path reads the archive files it would port.

## 4. The order

| round | track | items |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (Phase 87) | measurands | K2 + O5b + O5c + O5d + 1a + 1b |
| 2 | the loop | K4, I2, O3/M3; re-read L9 |
| 3 | measurands, continued | F |
| 4 | planner widenings | H-E6, then O7r and 2r |
| 5 | discourse state | 0b, D/0a, K3 |
| 6 | register against the world | C + H1 |
| 7 | the third sort | I1 + M1, then M2 |
| 8 | second readings | J2/H-X1, J1, J3 |
| 9 | retrieval | N1, L5, then N2, N5, I3 |

The first two rounds are the two that move *derive*, which the standing
target ([`PROJECT_DIRECTIVES.md`](../PROJECT_DIRECTIVES.md)) calls the scarce
one: the register lets a register value feed a derivation it could not feed
before, and the loop lets one answer choose the next step. Rounds 3 to 6 each
read something rounds 1 and 2 build. Rounds 7 to 9 are independent of the
first six and could be taken in any order; they are placed by how much they
would change what the machine answers.

**Round 1 was taken in Phase 87**
([`MEASURAND_REGISTER_STUDY.md`](MEASURAND_REGISTER_STUDY.md)): K2, O5b, O5c,
O5d and 1b are met; 1a (an offset row in the scale table) is not shipped,
because no register holds a reading in degrees Celsius to declare one for.
Round 2, the loop, is next.

## 5. What this does not settle

* **The owner's decisions.** K1, P-a and P-b are listed as gated, not as
  declined.
* **Outside material.** B, O1 and L4 need questions or a corpus nobody on the
  project wrote.
* **Order inside a round.** Each round still writes its own declarations and
  marks before its code, as every round has.
