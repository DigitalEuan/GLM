## 1. What `hypothesis_engine_v10.py` adds, and what it needs before it can be used

**The idea is good.** After the hypotheses are generated, the engine questions itself about each
one: state it, ask what would refute it, what it implies, how it connects, and what to do next.
This is the right shape for a GLM that has to steer its own investigation.

**As written, it cannot run here, and its answers are not generated** (`v10_audit.py` reproduces
the audit; output in `v10_audit.md`):

| check | finding |
|---|---|
| imports | `hypothesis_engine_v8` (class `EngineV8`) is not in the project or in `glm_repo.zip`. Four stage methods it calls (`run_prediction_chain`, `run_applications`, `run_gap_analysis`, `run_application_tests`) are not in the Round 38 engine either. |
| "no templated strings" | `speak(...)` is called at 24 places. In 15 of them the sentence is mostly fixed text chosen by keywords in the hypothesis text. Only the counts and the hypothesis text come from data. |
| numbers | Two fixed sentences carry numbers that are not in the ledger: "next instance estimated at D ≥ 275 million" and "with M=32 nothing below D=400,000". The ledger has the white diagonal at 1,420,878,968 (EMP, C370) and the predicted next doubling at 2,107,985,255 (EMP, C381). The fixed sentences would keep saying 275 million after these results. |

| mathematics | Three fixed "implication" sentences. One is wrong and one is only half right. Both are now settled in Lean (table below). |

## 2. v11: every answer is an action (`glm_dialogue.py`)

The dialogue loop of v10 is kept, and each question is now answered by doing something:

| question | v11 answer |
|---|---|
| state | assembled from the record only (status, evidence class, the largest index actually checked, the witness) |
| what would refute it? | **runs** the refutation: checks fresh ranges, doubling up to a per-puzzle budget. Pi2 statements are not sampled (no finite observation refutes them), and FINITE statements are already fully checked. |
| what does it imply? | answers only from `knowledge.json`, a graph of checked implications. Each edge is `follows`, `equivalent` or `does-not-follow` and names its Lean theorem or ledger claim. With no edge, it says so. |
| how does it connect? | parent and children, statements refuted by the same witness, statements about the same invariant |
| what next? | a fixed rule that is **executed**: read the counterexample (mine a law for the failing event with the puzzle's local predicates), narrow a refuted bound, propose "unbounded" after 3 refuted bounds, split a junction, flag a tie (and say when a checked equivalence makes it unsplittable), cite ledger facts, or hand the statement to the prover. New statements join the queue and are discussed in turn. |

A **grounding guard** checks every number of 3 or more digits in every utterance. It must have
been computed in this run or appear in the ledger, otherwise the line is marked UNGROUNDED. In the
Round 41 run, no line was.

## 3. What it did (`run_round41.py` → `r41_dialogue.md`, `r41_dialogue.json`)

**Rule 30.** The core sees only diagonals `D < 400`. The dialogue then chose all of the following
on its own (66 actions, about 30 s):

1. It extended the lift "white exactly at 2, 7, 28, 399" and **refuted it at 53207**. Reading the
   counterexample mined `white(D) ⟺ pattern(D−2) = pattern(D−1)`, the proved white criterion
   (`ldiag_white_iff_coincide`). A tied variant with an extra conjunct was flagged as equivalent
   by definition.
2. It extended "the period divides 8", **refuted it at 400**, and mined three laws for "the band
   period changes at D". The extended checks then **refuted `white(D−1)` alone at 53208**. The two
   survivors, `white(D−1) ∧ odd(D−2)` (the proved doubling law, `ldiag_doubles` and
   `ldiag_keeps_of_even`) and `white(D−1) ∧ odd(D−3)`, can never be split by data. Lean proves
   them equivalent (`doubling_law_tie_unsplittable`, new), and the dialogue says so instead of
   asking for more data.
3. It narrowed to "the period divides 16", **refuted it at 87867**, and narrowed again to 32. That
   statement survives to `D < 131072`, and the dialogue cites the ledger's EMP prediction (C381)
   that it fails at 2,107,985,255.
4. "Preperiod > D for D ≥ 20" survives to 16000. The dialogue states what follows (CERT,
   `centerCol_read_in_transient`) and what does **not** follow (Problem 1, by the new Lean
   counterexample).
5. Problem 1 (Pi2, open): it is equivalent to no finite-state observer reading the column being
   eventually always right. It does not by itself settle Problem 3 (Thue–Morse).

**Benchmarks** (the eight Round 39 problems, unchanged). The dialogue extends every survivor:
Euler's law to 80000, two squares to 120000, Collatz bounds to 400000. On Collatz it refutes the last
stopping-time bound at 106239 and narrows it. After that fourth refuted bound it **proposes "steps
is unbounded" by itself** (Round 39 did this by hand). It flags the midpoint tie `overflow` / `overflow ∧ hi_big` as equivalent, and
refuses to read "survives every sampled range" as "holds" (edge backed by `gcd_trap_refuted`).

## 4. Lessons for the GLM (portable)

1. **An utterance is a claim.** It should come from a record, an action, or a checked edge, and
   carry that provenance. Fixed sentences go stale: v10's 275-million estimate is an example.
2. **"What does it imply?" needs a checked implication graph.** Plausible implications are where
   false proofs come from. This round's two wrong v10 sentences would have claimed Problem 1, and
   would have conflated Problems 1 and 3.
3. **Know when data cannot decide.** Pi2 statements cannot be refuted by sampling, and some ties
   between laws are logical equivalences. In both cases the next step is the prover.
4. **Refutations drive the search.** Every new law in the Rule 30 run came from reading a
   counterexample the dialogue found by itself.
